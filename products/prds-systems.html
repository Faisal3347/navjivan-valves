PRDS Systems

Pressure Reducing and Desuperheating (PRDS) systems reduce high-pressure, high-temperature steam to a lower pressure and controlled temperature suitable for downstream process or heating use. Navjivan Valves supplies pilot-operated, self-contained, direct-acting and combined PRDS systems.

Product Overview

A PRDS combines pressure reduction with desuperheating (water injection to control outlet steam temperature) in a single integrated system, commonly used where boiler steam pressure and temperature are higher than what a downstream process or heat exchanger requires. Correctly specified PRDS systems improve energy efficiency, protect downstream equipment, and provide stable steam conditions to the process.

Types

Types of PRDS Systems

Pilot-Operated PRDS Systems

Use a pilot control mechanism to precisely regulate the main pressure-reducing valve, offering tight pressure control across varying load conditions.

Self-Contained PRDS Systems

Integrate the pressure-sensing and control function within the valve itself, without requiring external instrumentation or power, suitable for simpler installations.

Direct-Acting PRDS Systems

The simplest configuration, where the downstream pressure acts directly on a spring-loaded mechanism to regulate the valve position.

Combined PRDS Systems

Integrate pressure reduction and desuperheating functions into a single valve body, reducing installation footprint and piping complexity.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a PRDS system do?
A PRDS system reduces high steam pressure to a lower, controlled downstream pressure while also injecting water to reduce the steam temperature (desuperheating), delivering steam at the pressure and temperature the downstream process requires.
What's the difference between self-contained and pilot-operated PRDS systems?
A self-contained system senses and controls pressure within the valve itself, without external instrumentation — simple and robust for smaller installations. A pilot-operated system uses a separate pilot control loop for tighter, more responsive control, generally preferred for larger or more demanding steam systems.
